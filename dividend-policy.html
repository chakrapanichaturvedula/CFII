Session 8 · Types

Ways a firm can pay its shareholders

Cash

Regular dividend

Paid every year or quarter. In India, the board declares interim dividends; the final dividend is approved at the AGM.

Cash

Extra or special dividend

A one-off payment, labelled so investors do not expect it again.

Cash

Liquidating dividend

Part or all of the business is wound up and the capital returned.

Shares

Stock dividend (bonus issue)

No cash leaves the firm. Shares outstanding go up.

Kind

Dividend in kind

Products or discounts instead of cash. Rare.

Alternative

Share buyback

Cash out to the shareholders who choose to sell. More on this soon.

Session 8 · Price behaviour

On the ex-date the price drops by about the dividend

A buyer on the ex-date no longer gets the dividend, so the share is worth that much less. In a perfect world the drop equals the dividend.

In practice the drop is a little smaller than the dividend, because of taxes, and happens within minutes of the market opening.

Session 8 · The benchmark case

In a perfect market, dividend policy does not change firm value

  • Investors do not need dividends to turn shares into cash. They can sell shares.
  • So they will not pay more for a firm that pays more dividends.
  • Anyone who wants a different income stream can create it: a homemade dividend.

The assumptions

No taxes, no transaction costs, no uncertainty, and the firm's investment policy is set in advance and does not change with its dividend policy.

Bob reaches the same cash and the same wealth either way. That is why, in a perfect market, investors will not pay more for a firm because of its dividend policy.

Session 8 · Investment policy comes first

Never give up a positive NPV project to pay a dividend

The irrelevance result assumes the investment policy is fixed. A firm that cuts good projects to raise its dividend destroys value; the dividend itself was never the source of value.

Session 9 · Share repurchases

Why firms choose buybacks

Flexibility

A buyback does not set an expectation the way a dividend increase does. Shareholders also choose whether to sell.

Undervaluation

Management may think the share is cheap, and the buyback signals it.

Offset dilution

Shares bought back offset shares issued on employee stock options.

Executive compensation

Existing stock options are worth more when the firm repurchases instead of paying dividends.

Price support

Demand from the firm can hold up a falling share price.

Taxes

Depends on the country and year. In India the rules changed three times in a decade: next slide.

Session 9 · Personal taxes

How India has taxed payouts

Dividends
  • Up to March 2020: the company paid dividend distribution tax (DDT) of 15% plus surcharge and cess, about 20.6% effective. Most shareholders received dividends tax-free.
  • From April 2020: DDT abolished. Dividends are taxed in the shareholder's hands at their own income tax slab rate.
Buybacks of listed shares
  • 2013 to September 2024: the company paid a buyback tax, about 23.3% effective; the shareholder paid nothing.
  • October 2024 to March 2026: the whole buyback amount was treated as a dividend in the shareholder's hands.
  • From April 2026: taxed as capital gains for the shareholder (12.5% long term, 20% short term for listed shares), with an additional tax for promoters.

The general lesson from the textbook still holds: when dividends are taxed more heavily than capital gains, or capital gains can be deferred, investors prefer the firm to pay out less in dividends. Check the rules in force before you apply them to a real decision.

Session 9 · Firms with and without cash

With personal taxes, never issue shares just to pay a dividend

Without enough cash

Raise money from shareholders, pay the investment bankers, hand the money back as a dividend, and the government taxes it. Shareholders end up poorer.

With surplus cash

After taking every positive NPV project, the firm can take negative NPV projects, buy other companies, buy financial assets, or repurchase shares.

  • Taxes give managers a reason to look for other uses of cash before paying dividends.
  • But taxes are not strong enough to make firms stop paying dividends altogether.

Session 9 · Real-world factors

Why many firms still pay generous dividends

Desire for current income

Retirees and some funds want steady cash and prefer not to sell shares.

Behavioural finance

Dividends impose self-control: spend the dividend, never touch the capital.

Agency costs

Paying out free cash flow keeps it away from managers' pet projects.

Information

A dividend increase signals management's confidence in future earnings.

Session 9 · The clientele effect · Activity

Match each investor group to the payout it prefers

High tax bracket individualsDividends are taxed at their high slab rate; they prefer capital gains.
Low tax bracket individualsTaxes bite less, so some dividends are fine.
Tax-free institutionsPension and similar funds pay no tax on either, so they are indifferent and want steady income.
CorporationsCompanies receiving dividends often get tax relief on them, so they prefer high payouts.

Once every clientele is served, a firm cannot create value by switching its policy. It would only swap one set of shareholders for another.

Session 9 · What we know and do not know

Facts and a sensible policy

What we observe
  • Firms smooth dividends: they move slowly towards a target payout (Lintner).
  • Fewer firms pay dividends than before; buybacks have grown.
  • Dividend changes carry information to the market.
A sensible policy
  • Do not give up positive NPV projects to pay a dividend.
  • Avoid issuing shares to pay dividends.
  • Consider a buyback when there are few better uses for the cash.

Session 9 · RWJJS 19.9

Putting it all together

What we observe
  • Aggregate payouts are massive and have grown over time.
  • Dividends and buybacks are concentrated in a small number of large, mature firms.
  • Managers are reluctant to cut dividends, and they smooth them.
  • Prices react to unexpected changes in dividends.
  • Buybacks vary with temporary earnings.
General dividend guidelines
  • Over time, pay out all free cash flows.
  • Do not cut positive NPV projects to pay dividends or buy back shares.
  • Do not start dividends until free cash flows are substantial.
  • Set the regular dividend in line with a long-run target payout ratio.
  • Keep dividends low enough to avoid expensive future external financing.
  • Use buybacks to distribute temporary increases in cash.

Session 9 · Stock dividends and splits

More shares, same pie

You hold 100 shares at ₹600. Pick an action.

  • A small stock dividend is under about 20 to 25% of shares; a large one is above that.
  • A 2-for-1 split is economically the same as a 100% stock dividend.
  • The usual reason given: bring the price back to a more popular trading range.
