Leverage: a larger effect from a smaller force

Fixed costs act like a lever. Fixed operating costs magnify the effect of sales on profit. Fixed interest magnifies the effect of profit on EPS.

Operating leverage
DOL = %Δ EBIT / %Δ Sales

Comes from fixed operating costs.

Financial leverage
DFL = %Δ EPS / %Δ EBIT

Comes from fixed interest.

Total leverage
DTL = %Δ EPS / %Δ Sales

= DOL × DFL

Measuring financial and total leverage

Two short formulas

DFL = EBIT / (EBIT − Interest)Assumes no preference dividends.
DTL = DOL × DFL = Contribution / (EBIT − Interest)

Session 7 · Summary

What to remember

  • Fixed costs create leverage: operating fixed costs magnify sales into EBIT; interest magnifies EBIT into EPS.
  • Leverage is a double-edged sword: it magnifies falls exactly as it magnifies rises.
  • Leverage is greatest just above break-even and fades as the firm moves away from it.
  • Borrowing raises EPS only when the return on the investment exceeds the interest rate. The indifference EBIT is where the two are equal.
  • A firm with high operating leverage usually cannot afford high financial leverage too: DTL multiplies them.
