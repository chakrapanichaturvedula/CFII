Leverage: a larger effect from a smaller force

A cost that does not change when sales change works like a lever. A fixed operating cost makes profit swing more than sales. A fixed interest payment makes EPS swing more than profit.

Operating leverage
DOL = %Δ EBIT / %Δ Sales

Comes from fixed operating costs.

Financial leverage
DFL = %Δ EPS / %Δ EBIT

Comes from fixed interest.

Total leverage
DTL = %Δ EPS / %Δ Sales

= DOL × DFL

These three colours are used throughout the deck. Ross 14e does not cover DOL and DFL; the formulas here are the ones in standard Indian texts.

Financial leverage · Back to the bridges

Each bridge cost ₹100 lakh. A used only equity; B borrowed half

Face value ₹10 a share, tax rate 30%, EBIT ₹18 lakh for both.

EPS = (EBIT − Interest)(1 − t) / Number of shares
A: 10 lakh shares, no debt

B: 5 lakh shares, ₹50 lakh debt

Financial leverage · Mr B's bridge, interest 10%

Why DFL = EBIT / (EBIT − Interest)

1 · Today

EBIT 18, interest 5
EPS = (18 − 5) × 0.7 / 5
= ₹1.82

2 · EBIT rises 10%

EBIT 19.8, interest still 5
EPS = (19.8 − 5) × 0.7 / 5
= ₹2.07

3 · Compare the changes

%Δ EPS = (2.07 − 1.82) / 1.82
= 13.85%
DFL = 13.85% / 10% = 1.385

4 · Spot the formula

The extra 1.8 of EBIT all goes to shareholders, but on a base of 13, not 18:
1.8/13 = 10% × 18/13
DFL = EBIT / (EBIT − I) = 18 / 13

Mr A has no interest, so his DFL is 18/18 = 1: EPS moves exactly with EBIT. The tax rate and the number of shares cancel out, which is why they are not in the formula.

Financial leverage · ABC Ltd's new factory

₹10 crore to raise: how much debt?

Debt at 8%, tax 30%, shares at ₹10. Plan 1: all equity (1 crore shares). Plan 2: 25% debt (0.75 crore shares). Plan 3: 50% debt (0.5 crore shares).

Plan 1Plan 2Plan 3

Find the crossing: EPS of plan 1 = EPS of plan 2 → E × 0.7 / 1 = (E − 0.2) × 0.7 / 0.75 → 0.75 E = E − 0.2 → 0.25 E = 0.2 → E = ₹0.8 crore. That is 8% on ₹10 crore, the interest rate. Plan 3 crosses at the same point.

Combining the two

DTL = DOL × DFL

DTL = DOL × DFL = Contribution / EBIT × EBIT / (EBIT − I) = Contribution / (EBIT − I)EBIT cancels: sales feed EBIT, EBIT feeds EPS.

A 10% rise in sales lifts EBIT by DOL × 10%, and that lifts EPS by DFL × (DOL × 10%). So EPS moves by DOL × DFL × 10%.

XYZ Ltd needs ₹50 lakh for a call centre

Option 1

5,00,000 shares at ₹10

Option 2

2,00,000 shares at ₹10 and 3,000 debentures of ₹1,000

Option 3

1,00,000 shares at ₹10 and 4,000 debentures of ₹1,000

Debentures pay 12%, tax 35%. Fixed costs ₹2 lakh, variable cost 20% of sales. EBIT is ₹8 lakh.

Before computing: which option gives shareholders the highest EPS at EBIT ₹8 lakh?

EBIT ₹8 lakh is 16% on ₹50 lakh, above the 12% debt cost, so more debt raises EPS. Below ₹6 lakh the order reverses.

At EBIT ₹6 lakh all three options give the same EPS of ₹0.78: each is indifferent at a 12% pre-tax return on ₹50 lakh.

Session 7 · Summary

What to remember

  • Fixed operating costs magnify sales into EBIT; fixed interest magnifies EBIT into EPS; together they give total leverage.
  • Leverage is a double-edged sword: it magnifies falls exactly as it magnifies rises.
  • Leverage is greatest just above break-even and fades as the firm moves away from it.
  • Borrowing raises EPS only when the return on the investment exceeds the interest rate. The indifference EBIT is where the two are equal.
  • A firm with high operating leverage usually cannot afford high financial leverage too: DTL multiplies them.
