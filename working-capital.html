Session 10 · Tracing cash and NWC

Net working capital = current assets − current liabilities

Current assets

Expected to turn into cash within the year.

  • Cash and marketable securities
  • Accounts receivable
  • Inventory
Current liabilities

Expected to need cash payment within the year.

  • Accounts payable
  • Accrued wages
  • Taxes payable
Cash = Long-term debt + Equity + Current liabilities − Current assets other than cash − Fixed assets

So cash goes up when long-term debt or equity goes up, or when fixed assets or non-cash working capital go down. That gives us sources and uses of cash.

Session 10 · Computing the cycles

From the statements to the cycles

Inventory: ₹2,00,000 at the start, ₹3,00,000 at the end. Receivables: ₹1,60,000 to ₹2,00,000. Payables: ₹75,000 to ₹1,00,000. Net sales ₹11,50,000, cost of goods sold ₹8,20,000.

Inventory period

Average = ₹2,50,000
Turnover = 8,20,000 / 2,50,000 = 3.28
Period = 365 / 3.28 = 111.3 days

Receivables period

Average = ₹1,80,000
Turnover = 11,50,000 / 1,80,000 = 6.39
Period = 365 / 6.39 = 57.1 days

Payables period

Average = ₹87,500
Turnover = 8,20,000 / 87,500 = 9.37
Period = 365 / 9.37 = 38.9 days

Operating cycle168.4 days
Cash cycle129.5 days

The firm must finance its inventory and receivables for about 130 days. To cut that, look first at the inventory and receivables periods: both look long.

Session 11 · Short-term financial policy

Policy 1: how much to invest in current assets

Flexible

High current assets relative to sales

  • Large cash balances and marketable securities
  • Large inventories
  • Liberal credit terms
Restrictive

Low current assets relative to sales

  • Little cash, no marketable securities
  • Small inventories
  • Little or no credit sales

Flexible costs more to carry: money is tied up earning little. Restrictive risks shortages: lost sales, stock-outs, unhappy customers. The right level balances the two.

Session 12 · The short-term financial plan

Covering a temporary cash deficit

Unsecured

Bank line of credit

In India usually a cash credit or overdraft limit: draw and repay as needed, pay interest on what is used.

Secured by receivables

Factoring and bill discounting

Sell or pledge receivables for cash now. MSME suppliers can sell invoices on the TReDS platforms.

Secured by inventory

Inventory loans

Stock is hypothecated or pledged to the bank as collateral.

Market

Commercial paper

Short-term unsecured notes sold by large, highly rated companies.

Trade

Banker's acceptance

A bank guarantees a trade bill, which can then be sold at a discount.

For Pet Treats

A credit line covering the Q2 and Q3 deficit, repaid from the Q4 surplus, fits a temporary, seasonal need.
