Session 1 · Ratio analysis

Five families of ratios, five questions

Short-term solvency, or liquidity

Can it pay its bills this year?

Current, quick and cash ratios.

Long-term solvency, or financial leverage

How much is borrowed, and can it pay the interest?

Total debt ratio, debt-equity ratio, equity multiplier; times interest earned and cash coverage.

Asset management, or turnover

How hard do the assets work?

Inventory and receivables turnover, days' sales in inventory and in receivables, total asset turnover.

Profitability

How much does it earn?

Profit margin, EBITDA margin, ROA and ROE.

Market value

What does the market pay for it?

P/E, market-to-book and the EV multiple.

For every ratio ask

How is it computed? What does it measure? What unit is it in? What would make it better?

Session 2 · Du Pont analysis

Where does ROE come from?

ROE = Net income / Total equity
ROE = (NI / TA) × (TA / TE) = ROA × Equity multiplierMultiply and divide by total assets.
ROE = (NI / Sales) × (Sales / TA) × (TA / TE)Multiply and divide by sales as well.
ROE = Profit margin × Total asset turnover × Equity multiplier

Profit margin

Overall profitability: how much of each rupee of sales is left after all costs, including interest and taxes.

Asset turnover

Asset use efficiency: sales generated per rupee of assets.

Equity multiplier

Financial leverage: assets carried per rupee of equity, = 1 + D/E.

Session 2 · Benchmarks and limits

A ratio means little on its own

Compare it with
  • Itself over time (time-trend analysis): is performance improving?
  • Its peers (peer-group analysis): firms in the same industry, of similar size and business mix.
Watch out for
  • No theory says which ratios matter most.
  • Diversified firms have no clean peer group.
  • Different accounting choices and standards across firms and countries.
  • Different fiscal year ends; seasonal businesses.
  • One-time events that distort a single year.

Session 3 · Putting it together

Four levers set how fast a firm can grow

Sustainable growth = ROE × b / (1 − ROE × b), and ROE = PM × TAT × EM

Profit margin

Overall profitability. Higher margin, more retained earnings.

Asset turnover

Asset use efficiency. Fewer new assets per rupee of new sales.

Financial leverage

The debt ratio the firm chooses to hold.

Dividend policy

How much of each rupee earned is kept in the firm.

If the firm wants to grow faster than its sustainable rate, one of these must change: sell new shares, borrow more, pay out less, or run the business more efficiently.

Sessions 3 and 4

Models do not choose the policy for you

  • Planning models show the consequences of policies; they do not say which policy is best.
  • They simplify: fixed assets rarely grow smoothly with sales, and margins move.
  • Without a plan, though, the firm drifts. The plan forces management to make its assumptions explicit.

Quick check

Five questions

Prufrock's current ratio is 1.31 but its quick ratio is only 0.53. Why the gap?

Quick ratio drops inventory: (708 − 422) / 540. Inventory is ₹422 of ₹708 crore of current assets.

Days' sales in receivables of 30 means:

365 / 12.3 = 30 days of sales are still uncollected.

Two firms both earn ROE of 14%. Firm X: PM 15.7%, TAT 0.64, EM 1.385. Firm Y: PM 3%, TAT 2.5, EM 1.9. Which leans more on debt?

Y's equity multiplier of 1.9 means D/E of 0.9, against 0.385 for X.

Holding everything else fixed, which raises external financing needed?

Faster growth needs more new assets; the other two raise retained earnings and lower EFN.

Why is the sustainable growth rate higher than the internal growth rate?

Each rupee of retained earnings supports extra debt, so assets can grow faster.
